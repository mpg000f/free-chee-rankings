    How the playoff model works

    The top eight get in. Record comes first, then points for. A tie counts as half a win. If two teams somehow finish with the same record and the exact same points for, the table goes alphabetical and the simulation flips a coin. The Committee has its limits.

    Pick whatever results you want. Give yourself three wins, hand your rival three losses, or enter the exact score you’re already dreading. The model plays out the rest of the season 10,000 times with those picks locked in. Your playoff odds are how often you finish in the top eight. Your most likely finish is the spot you land in most often. The projected standings show one version of the finish using the favorites in the games you left open.

    We assume everyone sets their best lineup. The model takes the latest Yahoo rosters and uses weekly RotoWire projections to fill every legal starting slot, including flex and superflex. Once a player kicks off, their starter or bench spot is locked and any points already scored carry forward. If we can’t build an optimized projection, we use Yahoo’s set-lineup projection or the team’s season scoring average, nudged toward the league average. The more points already on the board, the less room there is for the score to move. As long as someone can still score, the game stays live in the model.

    Both sides of each matchup use the same projection source. We screen for current-week inactives and doubtful players, and can use a projected Yahoo free agent if there’s no healthy kicker or defense available on the roster. Each simulation gives a team a small strength adjustment that carries across its remaining games. Playoff odds above 99.9% or below 0.1% can mean the outcome never went the other way in our simulations.

    Title odds play out the whole bracket. The top eight go into the Week 15 quarterfinals. The highest remaining seed gets the lowest in Week 16, and the winners play for the title in Week 17. We use each week’s optimized projections. The higher seed advances on an exact score tie in the simulation.
