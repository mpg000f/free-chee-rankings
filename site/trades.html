How This Is Scored

Raw points can't compare players across positions, and in a superflex league that gets trades badly wrong. A quarterback and a wide receiver who both score 200 are not the same asset: the league has to fill 32 quarterback slots out of roughly 32 startable NFL starters, while the 40th-best receiver is still a useful one. Trading a good receiver for a middling quarterback can look even on points and be a clear loss.

So every player is measured against what a freely available player at his own position would have produced over the same weeks. That bar is set each season by ranking everyone at the position on scoring rate and reading off the man sitting one spot past the league's starting slots — the best you could realistically have streamed instead.

A side's grade is what it received above that bar minus what it gave away, per week left in the season, so a Week 12 swap isn't judged on the same scale as a Week 2 one. The bands are symmetric: in a two-team deal one side's A is the other's F, and an even trade lands both on C.

Two things worth knowing. Surplus floors at zero, because a player who came back below replacement could simply be dropped for one who didn't — the worst an acquisition can be worth is nothing, never less. And both halves are measured over the same window, whoever ended up rostering the player, so re-trading or cutting someone afterward doesn't rewrite the original deal.
