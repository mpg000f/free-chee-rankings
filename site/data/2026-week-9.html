Against the Field Through Week 8

Every week, we took your score and played it against everybody else’s. Beat 10 scores, go 10–5. Do that for eight weeks and you get 120 imaginary games, which is about how many the Committee needs before it’s comfortable admitting the schedule matters. Paul is 97–23 and leads the field by 18 wins. Justin is second at 79–41 and has a .500 record to show for it. Connor and Mikey are both 6–2 despite going 59–61 against the field. We’d ask them how they did it, but we assume they’d just tell us to look at the standings.

The Committee’s One-Question Press Conference

The league has granted the press one question per owner. We asked them to make it count.

Tier One — The Appeals Court

1
🔥Paul
Hello Darkness My Old Friend · 7–1
—

Hello Darkness beat Nick Wright by 0.72. The Committee was prepared to spend several paragraphs debating which of these two teams deserved the top spot, but Darkness saved us the trouble with the win. At 7–1 and alone atop the standings, Palm Beach has cemented itself as the epicenter of the league in 2026.

What we liked about Paul last week still holds. Walker and Jonathan Taylor give him two ways to ruin somebody’s afternoon before we even get to Burrow and Rodgers. The midseason report had Darkness fourth in starter carries plus targets and comfortably above the scoring line for that workload. There’s a reason we keep coming back to this team even when the receiver room looks like Grand Street afters on Sunday.

From the press: “Coach, if Walker and Taylor can’t keep up their torrid pace, what’s the pivot?”

Pelosi got waxed by Shough, 139.38–93.40. Drawing the high scorer is bad luck. Scoring 93.40 is how you make sure nobody has to spend much time feeling bad for you. Somehow, Mikey and the Rhino came out of the week with identical 6–2 records and identical 59–61 marks against the field.

Tier Three — The Committee Is Taking Calls

7
TJ
Boone · 5–3
▲ 2

Boone beat Ginny Sack 120.18–87.80 and moves up two spots. Beating Ginny is a lovely way to spend a Sunday. It’s also a game Boone could’ve won with 88 points, and he went ahead and scored 120 anyway. That’s a decent showing for a team the Committee was ready to read its last rites to when the quarterback room went to shit.

Starting Tanner McKee is about where the Committee thought things might get ugly. Instead, Tyler Allgeier has emerged as a guy Boone can actually use, and D’Andre Swift has been carrying enough of the load to cover for some of the guys going the other way. It helps that Christian Watson and Parker Washington have given TJ something he can count on at receiver. And we assume Baker is going to have to pass the ball at some point. If that happens, Boone might even get to have a good week without asking Swift to do everybody else’s job.

From the press: “Coach, how many more quarterbacks have to go down before you give Christian Watson a few reps under center?”
