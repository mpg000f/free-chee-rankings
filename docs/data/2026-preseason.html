Welcome back, gentlemen. After a highly successful offseason in which Papi faced relegation, a new member was added in his place and Jalen Hurts grew a mustache, we are back. This is the committee’s initial rankings for 2026 and in an effort to be as transparent, unbiased and ethical as possible the committee made an executive decision to forego any model inputs for this round. These rankings are based entirely on intense discussion and debate in the committee room. A record 43.1% of this year’s dollars were spent on quarterbacks. 21 of the 25 most expensive players in the entire auction were quarterbacks. “John” Tyler Shough cost $46. This is part of a league-wide trend of analytically minded owners allocating increased capital towards the ever-important quarterback position. As you can see in the table below, this is a continuation of a trend since the first year in 2022 where we as a league didn’t fully understand the importance of the quarterback. Now without further ado, we present the preseason rankings for 2026.

1
🔥TJ
Boone
▲ 8

After a disheartening end to 2025 in which Boone slid from a peak of #1 in the rankings in WK8 to 5-9 and out of the playoffs, he spent all offseason channeling that disappointment into optimizing his draft strategy. And the committee certainly believes it paid off. In the committee room, there was near-unanimous support for Boone as our preseason #1 team. He spent big on Jalen Hurts much to Connecticut’s chagrin, but he got Baker Mayfield at a bargain price of $35. This allowed him to spread his money around on a myriad of role players with solid upside. Derrick “King” Henry is back to kick Father Time’s teeth in again as he goes for his 5th consecutive finish as a top-8 RB. Beyond that, the committee loved the Jaylen Waddle, D’Andre Swift, Christian Watson and Parker Washington pickups at cost. Boone appears to have a floor of a playoff team and if things break right he’ll be the clear league favorite.

TJ spent a career high amount on QBs in this year’s auction. In the past, he’s been more of a bargain shopper for QBs but apparently saw value in spending heavier on a stalwart like Jalen Hurts this year.

Best Value: Tucker Kraft for $4. Tuck was really coming into his own last year before his season ending injury. He was averaging 14.6 points per game when he went out for the season last year. We fully expect Kraft to be a focal point for Matt LaFleur and the Packers.
Biggest Reach: Kaimi for $2

Deez
Pigs on the 7th Rank
▲ 2

The committee really liked the way Pigs executed his strategy. He decided wide receiver was where he wanted to compromise. A year after getting burned on a couple of expensive purchases (Tyreek and Terry McLaurin), Pigs reallocated his capital to more premium positions, including spending a career high $110 on the quarterback position. Lawrence and Mahomes are both high floor players who we would comfortably expect to finish in the top-15 among quarterbacks but they also have top-5 upside if things break right. Additionally, he locked down some solid if unspectacular backs in Josh Jacobs, David Montgomery and Jaylen Warren. His ceiling may hinge on just how dominant Brock “Bartholomew” Bowers can be as he is essentially the WR1 for Pigs.

Biggest Reach: No picks are egregious but the answer here is Josh Jacobs for $21. The notorious criminal has shouldered quite the workload over the past couple years and between wear and tear and his legal issues, he’s not the sure thing he used to be.
3
Mitch
Charles Dickens
▲ 2

Dickens has consistently stuck to his strategy and for the last two years it has rewarded him with 9-win seasons. He has been top-3 in QB spend in each draft since 2023. He went back to the well this year with two expensive QBs in Lamar Jackson ($60) and Drake Maye ($55) and then grabbed Chase and AJ Brown. This will be very much a stars and scrub approach where if any of those four players bust, this team will be bad. However, if they hit, the committee likes the tertiary pieces around that core. Some might chirp the fact that he took four Patriots. Some might turn out to be right. But the consensus in the committee room was that the Patriots are due to be a top-2 (not two) offense in the league. He spent either $2 or $3 on six different players looking for cheap options with some upside. With Jayden Higgins now out for the season with a torn ACL and Tyrone Tracy potentially supplanted by Najee “Jeans” Harris, two of his $3 players don’t look quite as good as they did on draft day.

Best Value: Cyrus “The Great” Allen for $1. Talk out of Chiefs camp is that this kid is electric. He’s decimating their rebuilt secondary and Mahomes has invited Allen to live with him to build chemistry heading into the season.
Biggest Reach: Easy to say now, but Jayden Higgins for $3 stinks. He’s out for the year and the committee loved this guy.

Free Paid
▼ 9

Some good and some bad here. Bo Nix for $48 is clean. He’s steady, just got a new WR1 and has been the QB7 overall in back-to-back seasons. Very good QB1. Jameson Williams is also a monster. He gets overlooked as Gibbs and Amon-Ra suck up all the oxygen around the Lions, but Williams has quietly been a top-20 receiver each of the last two seasons, and if Amon-Ra were to miss any time, he immediately becomes a slam dunk WR1. Now for the bad. The Watson-Cousins QB2 platoon is the living embodiment of the cardboard disgrace pile. I’m not sure Cousins could outrun JB Pritzker at this point. And many are questioning if doubling down on Eagles with Saquon and DeVonta Smith is the most prudent team-building philosophy. DeVonta is due for a big uptick in volume with AJ Brown gone, but he still has the 17th best QB in football throwing him the pigskin. The bigger concern is the new OC. Sean Mannion. This guy was a career third-string QB and has only been coaching for two seasons. He was the Packers QB coach last year. But he’s supposed to return the Eagles to the promised land? Hard to see it.

Perhaps the most balanced spender in the league, Free Paid put between 20% and 40% of his budget into the three big positions for the 2nd consecutive year. After a 13-1 regular season, Free Paid looks to replicate that success with a similar draft strategy.

Best Value: Tee Higgins $21. He’s going to have a monster year.
Biggest Reach: Kenny Gainwell $6. Free Paid was once one of the biggest Bucky Irving guys on the planet and now he’s spending $6 to acquire Bucky’s backup? Bucky will not take kindly to this disrespect.

Hello Darkness My Old Friend
▲ 2

This team has the potential to be very good, but generally the committee is fading some of these guys. Let’s start with the optimistic viewpoint. Armed with a state-of-the-art new training facility in Florida and access to top tier medical care that his competition can’t get in Hoboken, Darkness keeps his team healthy and rolls into the playoffs for the first time. Jonathan Taylor and Kenneth Walker form an elite RB duo while Burrow and Aaron Rodgers sling the rock like it’s 2021. Now the alternative. Jordyn Tyson and Chris Godwin are his two best receivers, and both are coming off injuries. Neither can stay healthy and Darkness is stuck starting milkmen at wide receiver. Rodgers looks washed up and begins doing weekly segments on Candace Owens’ podcast. Despite strong performances from Taylor-Walker-Burrow, the rest of the lineup never really coalesces, and Darkness misses the playoffs again. Committee sees this scenario as more plausible than the first.

In 2022, Darkness spent $168 combined on Ja’Marr Chase, Justin Jefferson and Mike Evans. That made it fairly challenging for him to fill out the rest of his roster. Since then, his spending habits have become much more in line with the rest of the league.

Best Value: Jalen Nailor $1. Somebody has to catch passes in Vegas, right? Might as well bet on the guy they gave $36M too. Also, people forget when Nailor decimated Rutgers.
Biggest Reach: Jordyn Tyson $5. The committee loves Tyson and thinks he’s going to be nasty long-term, but he’s already having hamstring issues that are expected to rule him out for two months.
