The Injury Ledger

We promised Charles Dickens an accounting, so here it is. For every player drafted since 2022 we counted the games he missed to injury — injured reserve, the physically-unable-to-perform list, or an official designation followed by a did-not-play — and charged his owner the share of the auction price those weeks represent. Byes do not count. Then we did the part that actually matters: we subtracted what the waiver wire would have handed back. Replacement level is the median of the top five unrostered players at that position each week, so a receiver who goes down and is replaced by a comparable body off the wire costs his owner almost nothing.

That subtraction is the entire story. League-wide this season the gross bill is $415 and the net bill is $200, meaning 52 cents of every injury dollar was recoverable by anybody paying attention on Tuesday morning. And the recovery is almost purely positional. Since 2022, receivers and tight ends have cost this league exactly nothing net across 641 player-games, because somebody comparable is always sitting there. Running backs give back about two-thirds. Quarterbacks give back essentially nothing: in a sixteen-team superflex the best available free-agent quarterback has averaged about one point a week. Thirteen of the fifteen most expensive injuries in league history are quarterbacks.
